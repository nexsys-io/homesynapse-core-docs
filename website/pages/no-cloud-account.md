# No cloud account. Really.

**{{productName}} requires no account, no registration, and no phone-home — not as a privacy policy, but as an architecture you can verify.** The control plane is your hardware. Unplug the WAN cable and try it: everything still works, because nothing was ever routed through us.

- **No account to create.** First run asks you to sign in to nothing. There is no server-side "you" to create, suspend, or lose.
- **No cloud dependency to keep working.** Automations evaluate on your hardware. Your home does not degrade when a data center has a bad day — or when a company does.
- **Nothing leaves your home unless you send it.** Local-first is the default state, not a premium tier or a checkbox.
- **What happens to your home if we disappear? Nothing.** That is the point, and the industry has already run the experiment the other way: when cloud-dependent platforms shut down, working homes stopped working. Ours can't be shut down remotely, because there is nothing remote to shut down.

For readers in the EU, the regulatory direction now points the same way — the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854, applicable since September 2025) made access to connected-device data a legal expectation. {{productName}}'s answer is structural: your data never left your home in the first place.

The full dossier — the account-dependency matrix across platforms, and the shutdown history with sources — is being written.
